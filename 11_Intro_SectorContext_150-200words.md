# INTRODUCTION — Sector/Organisational Context
**Target: 150–200 words. Opens with the financial system broadly, narrows through centralised → decentralised, then into the specific topic.**

---

Financial systems have historically relied on centralised intermediaries — banks, clearing houses, custodians — to verify counterparties, hold collateral, and enforce repayment. Decentralised finance (DeFi) proposes an alternative architecture, removing that intermediary layer and, with it, the compliance pre-qualification and credit-scoring barriers that have defined access to centralised finance (Scharfman, 2021; Schär, 2021; Chen and Bellavitis, 2020). That shift has been rapid rather than gradual: DeFi's market capitalisation stood at under $700 million in December 2020 and had passed $130 billion within eighteen months (Scharfman, 2021). Lending is the sector's clearest proof of concept — Qin *et al.* (2021) find four protocols alone, Aave, Compound, MakerDAO, and dYdX, hold over 85 per cent of Ethereum's lending market. Removing the intermediary has not removed the risk it used to absorb; it has relocated that risk into the code itself. Zhou *et al.* (2023), studying 181 real-world attacks, document at least $3.24 billion in losses, and Chaliasos *et al.* (2024) trace a substantial share of this persistence to one correctable gap: security tools are evaluated in isolation from one another. This dissertation begins there — not with whether existing tools work, but with what happens when two of them disagree.

## Why this version, briefly

The opening sentence names what centralised intermediaries actually *do* — verify, custody, enforce — before DeFi is introduced as the thing that removes them, so the reader understands the trade-off being made, not just the technology being described. Scharfman (2021) — a Springer-published book, accessed via the university's own ProQuest subscription — anchors both the intermediary-removal claim and the concrete growth trajectory ($700m to $130bn in eighteen months), giving the paragraph a book-length academic source alongside the two journal papers (Schär, 2021; Chen and Bellavitis, 2020) that were already doing that work. Qin *et al.* (2021) and Zhou *et al.* (2023) are the same peer-reviewed sources already used to replace industry-blog citations elsewhere in the dissertation (see 14_Organisation_Sector_Analysis), kept consistent here rather than reintroducing website figures. The line "it has relocated that risk into the code itself" is still the hinge of the paragraph — it converts the centralised/decentralised framing into the actual argument (removing intermediaries doesn't remove risk, it moves where the risk lives), which is what justifies everything that follows about validating that code's behaviour.
